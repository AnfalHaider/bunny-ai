## Engines: Claude Code and Codex inside Bunny

- **Settings → Engines** lets a Code-mode chat run on **Claude Code** or **Codex CLI** instead of Bunny's own engine, with Bunny's chat, steps and permission cards around it.
- Each engine is downloaded with one click (about 380–390 MB), checked against a known fingerprint, and can be updated to tested versions or rolled back from its row.
- Each engine has **Local** (on the models you set up in Bunny) or **Account** (signed in with the maker), with the recommended one marked: Claude Code recommends Account; Codex recommends Local. Codex's Account option comes later.
- Pick the engine for a chat before its first message; a small label shows which engine a chat uses.
- Bunny checks every step an engine wants to take against your Bunny rules. If an engine ever acts without that check, Bunny stops it and offers **restricted mode**: the engine works in a copy of your folder with commands off, and you choose **Keep the changes** or **Discard** at the end.
- **Use Bunny's engine** (on by default): turn it off to let chats follow the engine's own rules, which you set in Settings → Engines. Bunny still protects its own data, its keys and the engines' sign-ins, and every question the engine asks reaches you as a card. Codex cards can also offer **Allow for this chat** for that exact command.
- Engines respect your spending limit, and on a device with Everything their tools appear in Activity.
- The first Codex chat in each project can take a few minutes to start while Windows prepares its sandbox; after that, commands start at once.

## Search this PC

- Ask Bunny (or the Quick ask bar) to find files on this PC through Windows Search. Turn it on in **Settings → Capabilities**, and choose whether Bunny reads the best matches and answers, or just shows what it found.
- Answers cite the files they used. Some folders are always excluded (Bunny's own data, AppData, Windows, Program Files, key folders and more), and you can add your own.
- After a file's text is read, anything that writes, runs or goes online asks you first, even on Auto. Programs and shortcuts only offer **Show in folder**.

## Quick ask can search the web

- The Quick ask bar now searches the web and reads pages, answering in the bar with its sources. It reads only addresses you typed or links its own search found, never your PC or home network.
- In chats, if a model's provider rejects Bunny's tools, Bunny keeps its own web search and drops only the connectors, so the model no longer claims it has no internet.

## Devices

- The banner across the top is gone. A small chip in the top bar shows **Open to devices · N**, turns red and names a device that has Everything, and shows new events such as "New: Pixel 8 signed in". Tap it for the devices list and Activity.
- A phone that is turned away at sign-in now says why (wrong passcode, address changed, too many tries, signed out).
- Bunny's window no longer waits for the network check when it starts.

## Phones

- The chat box is no longer cut off on signed-in phones, and it rises above the keyboard.
- The top bar becomes ☰ · title · chip · ⋯, with everything else in a sheet of large rows.
- Settings on a phone is a list of sections with search.
- Menus stay on screen, landscape keeps room for the chat, and buttons are easier to tap.

## Everything says Bunny

- Plugins and skills made for Claude now read as Bunny's everywhere you or the AI see them, and commands that take what you type after them work. Code inside skills is left exactly as written.

## Tidier lists

- Plugins, connectors, skills, servers, hooks and schedules each take one row, with the switch on the same line and other actions under ⋯.

Copies update on their own (**Restart to update**, or check for updates in **Settings → About**).
