For approval

In short

A new block in Settings → Use Bunny in a browser, Outside your home network: three routes (Off, Tailscale Funnel recommended, Your own HTTPS address) and three switches the owner asked for: Keep devices signed in, Everything stays on and Stay open to the internet after a restart. Internet devices start at Chats like any device and can be given Everything from the window. There is no banner and no notice anywhere: only the chip in the top bar. It carries the state in words and, when something new happens, switches to the event's words with a count until it is clicked (Security's BN-6 to BN-9). Rows use the TIGHT-ROWS density. Click the state buttons in section 1 to see every state.

2 · The device-name warning (before HTTPS is turned on)

This PC's name will be public

Turning on HTTPS for Tailscale puts pc-4821.tail7c3d9.ts.net in public certificate logs, and it can't be removed.

If the name says who you are or where you live, rename this device in Tailscale first. After you continue, Tailscale asks you to allow HTTPS in your browser.

Rename in Tailscale opens Tailscale's machines page in the browser, then Bunny reads the status again. Continue goes on to Tailscale's own consent page. The first focus is on Rename, not Continue, and Esc cancels. The dialog never suggests turning HTTPS off and on again (Let's Encrypt can make you wait a day or more).

5 · Activity

Names, never addresses. A visitor's address is used to count tries and is never stored or shown (OS-10). The tailnet name appears on route-on, route-off, reopened-at-start and name-confirmed rows. Each row carries "Internet" when it came from outside; home-network rows are unchanged. Each grant of Everything and each drop back to Chats is recorded, for internet devices too.

Passcode. 16 or more characters is needed to turn the route on. If the saved passcode is shorter, the passcode field opens first (state "Passcode too short" above) with a made-up 20-character suggestion; the same passcode then applies at home too.

Drawn to the owner's two decisions
