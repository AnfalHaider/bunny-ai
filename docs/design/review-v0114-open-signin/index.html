In short

A new block in Settings → Use Bunny in a browser, Outside your home network, with three choices: Off (always the state at start), Tailscale Funnel (recommended) and Your own HTTPS address. Everything from the internet stays at Chats, there is no Everything choice for those devices, and while the route is on a warning chip in the top bar says so and cannot be dismissed. Rows use the TIGHT-ROWS density. Click the state buttons in section 1 to see every state.

2 · The device-name warning (before HTTPS is turned on)

This PC's name will be public

Turning on HTTPS for Tailscale puts pc-4821.tail7c3d9.ts.net in public certificate logs, and it can't be removed.

If the name says who you are or where you live, rename this device in Tailscale first. After you continue, Tailscale asks you to allow HTTPS in your browser.

Rename in Tailscale opens Tailscale's machines page in the browser, then Bunny reads the status again. Continue goes on to Tailscale's own consent page. The first focus is on Rename, not Continue, and Esc cancels. The dialog never suggests turning HTTPS off and on again (Let's Encrypt can make you wait a day or more).

5 · Activity

Names, never addresses. A visitor's address is used to count tries and is never stored or shown (OS-10), so no row holds one. The tailnet name appears only on route-on, route-off and name-confirmed rows. Each row carries "Internet" when it came from outside; home-network rows are unchanged.

Passcode. 16 or more characters is needed to turn the route on. If the saved passcode is shorter, the passcode field opens first (state "Passcode too short" above) with a made-up 20-character suggestion; the same passcode then applies at home too. The words under the field: "Anyone on the internet can try this. Use the made-up one, or a long phrase."
